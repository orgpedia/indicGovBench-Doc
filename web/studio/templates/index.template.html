IndicGovBench-Orders: Document Transcription Benchmark for Government Orders

Most OCR benchmarks do not adequately represent real-world scanned documents. Very few include scanned documents in Indian languages, and almost none focus specifically on scanned government documents in Indian languages. IndicGovBench-Orders is designed to address this gap.

We have curated {{TOTAL_DOCS}} government orders across {{LANGUAGES_CONJUNCTION}}, collectively covering a diverse range of document characteristics and challenges found in real-world government orders. These include varied table structures, combinations of handwritten and computer-generated text, stamps, signatures, and other document elements. The benchmark currently evaluates two tasks: OCR/transcription and table extraction.

We call this benchmark IndicGovBench-Orders. [Link to paper] provides a more detailed description of the benchmark, its construction, and its comparison with existing benchmarks.

Given the scale of government and the sheer volume of documents generated every day, there is a strong need for reliable automated techniques for extracting information from these documents. IndicGovBench-Orders is our first step towards building benchmarks that evaluate these capabilities on real-world government documents.

Over the longer term, we plan to expand the benchmark to cover 10 Indian languages, a much broader range of government documents—including Gazettes, land records, legislative documents, financial records, and electoral rolls—and additional document-understanding tasks. Our ultimate goal is to build IndicGovBench-Doc, a benchmark that covers the breadth of documents generated by governments and the different tasks required to understand them.

We envision IndicGovBench-Doc becoming an important benchmark for evaluating progress in document intelligence, much as established benchmarks have helped drive progress in areas such as question answering, speech-to-text, speaker diarization, financial analysis, and Text-to-SQL. Our goal is to provide researchers and developers with challenging, real-world documents that reflect the complexity of documents encountered in practice.

Metric Definitions
How we score transcription accuracy, reading order, and table extraction.
Open Metrics Tutorial ↗
We evaluate models across character accuracy, reading order sequence, and structural table similarity. To see step-by-step examples, edge cases, and how text is cleaned before scoring, see the Metrics Tutorial ↗.
Composite Score ▲ Higher is better
An equal blend (1/3 each) of character accuracy (TextCER), reading order (e2eNED), and table accuracy (TEDS). Gives a single overall score from 0 to 1 (or 0–100%).
TextCER (Norm CER) ▼ Lower is better
Character error rate on document text (lower is better). Measures missing, extra, or misspelled characters after standardizing spelling and Unicode.
e2eNED (Edit Distance) ▼ Lower is better
Reading-order error rate (lower is better). Checks if the text flows in the right sequence across columns and sections, penalizing scrambled or skipped blocks.
TEDS (Table Similarity) ▲ Higher is better
Table accuracy score (higher is better). Evaluates both the layout (rows, columns, merged cells) and the text inside each cell.
Cost (₹ INR / Page) ▼ Lower is better
Average per-page processing cost in Indian Rupees (₹). Models with asynchronous Batch API support (50% discount) are marked with a green Batching tag. Prices quoted in the leaderboard reflect real-time inference pricing.
